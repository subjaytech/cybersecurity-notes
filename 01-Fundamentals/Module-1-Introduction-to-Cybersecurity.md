# Module 1 - Introduction to Cybersecurity

## Overview

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting systems, networks, applications, and data from unauthorized access, attacks, and damage.

Cybersecurity protects:

- Individuals
- Businesses
- Governments
- Critical Infrastructure

---

## Online Identity vs Offline Identity

Our online identity consists of information shared through:

- Social media
- Websites
- Email
- Online purchases
- Search history

Unlike offline information, online data spreads quickly and is difficult to remove once exposed.

---

## Case Study: Bybit Hack (February 2025)

### What Happened

Attackers stole approximately 400,000 ETH by manipulating the transaction approval process during a routine transfer.

### Attack Method

The Safe{Wallet} interface used for multi-signature approvals was compromised, causing legitimate signers to unknowingly approve a malicious transaction.

### Attribution

Lazarus Group.

### Lessons Learned

- Multi-signature wallets are not immune to attacks.
- Trusted interfaces should always be verified.
- Human verification remains one of the strongest security controls.

## Stuxnet

Stuxnet is one of the most sophisticated malware campaigns ever discovered.

Key characteristics:

- Targeted industrial control systems
- Exploited multiple zero-day vulnerabilities
- Used modular architecture
- Designed for physical sabotage instead of financial gain

---

## Key Takeaways

- Cybersecurity protects more than computers.
- Human error remains a major attack vector.
- Advanced attacks often combine technical exploits with social engineering.
